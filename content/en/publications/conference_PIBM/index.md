**Abstract**: The strong optical scattering of Human skin is still a great challenge in the field of non-invasive in-vivo imaging. To investigate the depth-resolved distribution of photons backscattered from deep Human skin tissues, we report a Monte-Carlo simulation model in a standard spatially offset configuration. In the simulation initializations, human skin was modelling to be composed of three layers (epidermis, papillary dermis, and reticular dermis), and each layer possessed inherent photon weights for backscattering intensity measurements. The simulation results clearly showed the total backscattered intensity varied with the increase of spatial offset, indicating depth-related photon information was encoded into spatial transients. We developed a nonlinear signal unmixture algorithm to recover the depth-resolved backscattered intensities through comparing the photon weights at different offsets. The proposed method paves the way for designing non-invasive depth-sensitive optical imaging and spectroscopic instrumentations for Human skin.
